SPICE

Connecting stellar feedback in the first galaxies and cosmic reionisation

SPICE is a radiation-hydrodynamics simulation programme exploring how galaxies form and how their radiation reshapes the Universe. It connects small-scale physics with cosmic-scale evolution in a single coherent framework.

The simulations follow how stars form, generate ionising photons, and alter their surrounding gas, while also tracing how these local processes contribute to the large-scale topology of reionisation across cosmological volumes.

Visualization

The SPICE simulations generate a wide range of visual material, from large-scale ionisation structure to galaxy-scale zooms and time-evolution sequences. This section can work both as a gallery and as a flexible home for static images, embedded videos, and linked media.

Options for adding media:

PNG / JPG: standard image files for maps, slices, or publication figures.

MP4: use the <video> element for local movies exported from the simulations.

YouTube / Vimeo: embed externally hosted movies using an <iframe> when files are too large for the repository.

Linked media pages: each image or movie can link out to a dedicated gallery page, project page, paper page, or downloadable full-resolution asset.

Large-scale ionisation maps

Wide-field renderings of ionised regions across cosmological volumes, showing how reionisation topology traces galaxy clustering and large-scale density structure.

Galaxy-centred zooms

High-resolution views of individual halos and their surrounding gas, useful for illustrating feedback, escape channels, and the local structure of radiation-driven evolution.

Density and temperature slices

Cross-sectional views through the simulation volume highlighting the multiphase interstellar and intergalactic medium, thermal structure, and evolving fronts.

Time-evolution movies

Animated sequences following galaxies, ionisation fronts, and the large-scale growth of reionised regions across redshift.

Comparative visualisations

Side-by-side images comparing different runs, feedback prescriptions, or resolutions, helping to show how physical assumptions alter the resulting structures.

Figure-ready scientific outputs

Publication-style panels, annotated composites, and presentation-ready visuals for papers, talks, and outreach material.
