Overview

Scientific context

This project studies how ionising radiation escapes from high-redshift galaxies, focusing on feedback-driven porosity, bursty star formation, and the evolving structure of the interstellar medium.

Escape fractions are not imposed externally but emerge from the interaction between stellar feedback, star formation history, and gas geometry, producing highly time-variable leakage into the surrounding medium.

Results

Key findings

Escape is highly time-variableShort-lived feedback episodes can temporarily open channels through which ionising radiation escapes far more efficiently.
Low-mass systems can contribute stronglyEven relatively faint galaxies may become important sources during bursty phases of star formation and outflow-driven clearing.
Geometry mattersThe anisotropic structure of dense gas strongly shapes when and where radiation can propagate beyond the galaxy.
