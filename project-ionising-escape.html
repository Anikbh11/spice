Project 01 · Radiation escape

Ionising escape from early galaxies

How feedback-driven porosity, bursty star formation, and the changing structure of interstellar gas regulate the radiation available to reionise the Universe.

StatusPublished research
ScaleGalaxies and circumgalactic gas
FocusEscape fraction and variability
MethodsMatched feedback models

01 · Question

From stars to the intergalactic medium.

What lets ionising photons escape?

The production of ionising radiation is only part of the reionisation problem. Photons must also travel through a dense, structured, and rapidly changing interstellar medium.

SPICE follows radiation transport together with star formation and feedback, allowing the escape fraction to emerge from the evolving gas distribution rather than being imposed as an external parameter.

The matched feedback runs make it possible to examine how the cadence and energy of stellar feedback affect low-density channels, temporal variability, and the relative contribution of galaxies across halo mass.

02 · Interpretation

Mechanisms explored by the project.

Physical picture

01

Escape is time-dependent

Short-lived episodes of star formation and feedback can rearrange the surrounding gas and change when radiation can propagate beyond a galaxy.

02

Geometry matters

Dense gas is not distributed uniformly. Direction-dependent low-density paths can create highly anisotropic radiation escape.

03

Feedback links scales

Processes acting inside galaxies change the surrounding radiation field and the larger-scale progress of reionisation.
